# Competitor and SERP Analysis

## Objective

Explain who competes in organic search, what result formats satisfy the target intent, and where the site has a defensible opportunity.

Read `../seo-director/specialist-contract.md`. Use business competitors recorded by `project-intake`, but identify organic competitors separately. Do not redefine business strategy or assign final priorities.

When executing against Phase 3 snapshots, read `references/procedure.md` and use its deterministic runtime contract. Do not substitute an ad hoc analysis when compatible approved datasets are available.

## Analyze

- Business competitors versus organic search competitors
- Publishers, marketplaces, directories, forums, video, local packs, and other result types
- Dominant intent and page format for representative queries
- SERP features, freshness, locality, authority, and brand/entity patterns
- Competitor architecture, content depth, evidence, trust, and link-worthiness
- Gaps the target can address with genuine differentiation
- Feasibility based on relevance, authority, evidence, and operational fit

Sample queries across the intake-approved markets, languages, devices, and locations. Record when personalization, localization, or limited sampling reduces confidence.

Do not invent competitor traffic, rankings, authority, or market share. Treat third-party estimates as directional and cite their source/date. Treat observed result composition as a time-stamped sample, not a Google requirement, permanent ranking rule, or guarantee that reproducing a page format will rank.

## Deliverable-specific output

Provide:

- Competitor classification table
- Representative SERP-pattern table
- Winning page-type and intent observations
- Defensible gaps and barriers
- Inputs needed by keyword, content, authority, and GEO/AEO workstreams

Use `SERP-##` IDs. Return findings to `seo-director`.
